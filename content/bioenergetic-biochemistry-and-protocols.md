# Bioenergetic Biochemistry, Therapeutics, & Protocol Manual

This document consolidates the fundamental biochemistry, thermodynamic concepts, endocrine profiles, modern pathology models, and active therapeutic protocols of the bioenergetic framework.

---

## 1. Core Philosophy & Bioenergetic Fundamentals

### The Energy Paradigm
* **Declining Baseline Metabolic Rate (BMR):** Modern research (e.g., J.R. Speakman) indicates a progressive decrease in global BMR over the last several decades. Chronic caloric restriction downregulates BMR long-term, which can trigger rapid fat regain during refeeding (the fat overshoot model). Obesity is a survival-driven adaptation to a depressed metabolic rate. Having a slightly higher body fat percentage with a roaring, high BMR is healthier than being lean with a suppressed metabolism.
* **The Essential Fatty Acid (EFA) Myth:** Supposed EFA deficiencies demonstrated in historic trials were actually B-vitamin deficiencies. Completely removing EFAs accelerates metabolic rate to such a degree that B-vitamin requirements spike dramatically.
* **Therapeutic Philosophy:** Standard medical interventions (e.g., extreme fasting, aggressive LDL lowering, rigid restriction of simple sugars) attempt to slow a compromised engine so it breaks slower. The bioenergetic goal is to change the environment (minimizing seed oils, plastics, and EMFs) to unblock the engine so it runs efficiently.
* **Historical Reference Point:** According to USDA data, the average male in 1939 consumed approximately 4,500 calories daily while maintaining a lean, muscular physique—reflecting a vastly superior baseline metabolic rate.

### Redox Balance: The Cellular Tug-of-War
* **The Efficiently Oxidized State:**
  * High NAD⁺/NADH, FAD/FADH₂, and active ATP consumption.
  * The Electron Transport Chain (ETC) is fully functional; NADH is oxidized cleanly.
  * ATP consumption creates the ADP demand that drives forward proton flow.
  * Adaptive mitochondrial uncoupling prevents over-reduction of the ETC, lowers membrane potential to minimize ROS, and generates therapeutic heat.
* **The Pathological Reduced State:**
  * High NADH/NAD⁺, FADH₂/FAD, and low ATP demand.
  * Excess NADH stalls glycolysis, the Pyruvate Dehydrogenase (PDH) complex, and the TCA cycle.
  * Absence of ADP backs up electrons, elevating membrane potential and triggering Reverse Electron Transport (RET).
  * RET is the primary source of pathological Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) leakage, occurring predominantly during fatty acid oxidation.

## 2. Mitochondrial Respiration & Electron Transport Chain (ETC)

### Respiration Dynamics
* Cells maintain an incredibly tight 8-to-12 second reserve of ATP and creatine phosphate.
* Under aerobic conditions, pyruvate enters the mitochondria and is converted to acetyl-CoA by Pyruvate Dehydrogenase (PDH), releasing carbon dioxide (CO₂) and converting NAD⁺ to NADH.
* NADH donates its electrons to Complex I, regenerating NAD⁺ for glycolysis and the TCA cycle.

### The ETC Power Stations & Target Supports
* **Complex I (NADH:Ubiquinone Oxidoreductase):** Primary entry point for carbohydrate-derived electrons. Dependent on B-vitamins (B1/Thiamine for NADH generation, B2/Riboflavin as a structural component). Blockages cause electron backup, causing severe reductive stress.
* **Complex II (Succinate Dehydrogenase):** Primary entry point for fatty acid-derived electrons (via FADH₂). SDH activity can be acetylated and impaired in obesity. Directly fueled by disodium succinate.
* **Complex III (Cytochrome c Reductase):** Accepts electrons from both Complex I and II via CoQ10. Heavily impaired by statin medications which block CoQ10 synthesis. Blockages cause electron leakage. Can be bridged with quinones (Vitamin K2 MK-4, ubiquinone) and Vitamin C.
* **Complex IV (Cytochrome c Oxidase):** Decisive oxygen-reducing step. Highly dependent on copper. Directly stimulated and upregulated by red and near-infrared light.
* **Complex V (ATP Synthase):** Uses the proton gradient to synthesize ATP.

### Safety Valves & Modulators
* **Nicotinamide Nucleotide Transhydrogenase (NNT):** The mitochondrial pressure-release valve. Transfers electrons from NADH to NADP⁺, regenerating NAD⁺ (alleviating reductive stress) and creating NADPH (fueling antioxidant defenses). Overloaded acetyl-CoA acetylates and impairs NNT.
* **Mitochondrial Uncoupling:**
  * *Adaptive (Good):* Pokes holes in the proton gradient to vent energy as heat, reducing electron pressure and ROS. Stimulated by saturated fat oxidation. Vitamin E, niacinamide, glycine, caffeine, and low-dose aspirin act as mild uncouplers.
  * *Pathological (Bad):* Occurs in pre-reduced, energy-starved cells, forcing them to work harder and worsening reductive stress.
  * *Exogenous Uncouplers:* CCCP achieves ~4% uncoupling. BAM15 is considered an upgraded, safer analog of DNP. DNP (2,4-Dinitrophenol) has a 36-hour half-life; excessive accumulation leads to lethal hyperthermia (>39°C / 102.2°F).

## 3. Fuel Selection & Satiety (The Randle Cycle)

### The Randle Cycle
* Flooding the blood with free fatty acids (FFAs) via high fat intake, adrenaline, or cortisol forces fat oxidation. This generates acetyl-CoA and NADH, activating Pyruvate Dehydrogenase Kinase (PDK). PDK phosphorylates and shuts down the Pyruvate Dehydrogenase (PDH) gateway.
* PDH inhibition locks glucose out of mitochondrial respiration, shunting pyruvate to lactate (reductive stress).
* To limit fat availability to the cell, utilize anti-lipolytic agents (niacinamide, aspirin, biotin) or direct inhibitors of fat oxidation (Meldonium).

### Carbohydrates: The Clean-Burning Fuel
* **Thyroid Conversion:** Adequate glucose is mandatory for the deiodinase enzymes to convert T4 to active T3. High-carb, low-fat diets (<20% fat cals) prevent PPAR-alpha signaling, lower FFAs, raise NAD⁺ via PDH, and increase metabolic rate.
* **Fructose vs. Glucose:** Fructose is selectively processed by the liver. In a healthy liver (low PUFA, low endotoxin), fructose is converted to glucose (~50-60%), lactate (~15-25% used by heart/muscle), and liver glycogen (~15-20%), with <1-3% undergoing *de novo* lipogenesis. Fructose infusions yield ~360% more hepatic glycogen than glucose.
* **Safe Starches:** Use low-BCAA, safe starches (white rice, peeled potatoes, sweet potatoes, tapioca/cassava, taro, yam, chestnuts, plantains, butternut squash, arrowroot).

### Fats: Saturation vs. Instability
* **Saturated Fatty Acids (SFAs):** Stearic acid, butter, ghee, and coconut oil are stable fuels lacking weak double bonds. High tissue stearic acid strongly correlates with lower body fat. Stearic acid is metabolized to Stearoylethanolamide (SEA), which suppresses SCD1, lowers TNF-alpha, and resists obesity.
* **Monounsaturated Fatty Acids (MUFAs):** Oleic acid (olive oil) acts as a seasonal hibernation signal. MUFAs upregulate SCD1, activate PPAR-alpha via OEA (oleoylethanolamide), increase endocannabinoids (causing appetite stimulation and metabolic slowdown), and generate high postprandial triglyceride spikes. Palm kernel oil contains only around 12% MUFA, whereas palm oil contains 39% MUFA.
* **Polyunsaturated Fatty Acids (PUFAs):** Highly unstable, containing weak double bonds prone to lipid peroxidation. They preferentially liberate during lipolysis, activate the PPAR-alpha winter switch, agonize estrogen receptors, and directly inhibit T4-to-T3 thyroid conversion at every level.
* **PUFA Depletion:** Fat-free diets can rapidly deplete serum PUFAs in days and tissue PUFAs in about 30 days. Adding coconut oil (even at 45% cals) slows tissue PUFA depletion because exogenous fat reduces the need to mobilize stored adipose tissues.

### The BCAA Problem & Amino Acid Balancing
* **BCAAs (Leucine, Isoleucine, Valine):** High circulating BCAAs are strongly causal in insulin resistance and obesity. High dietary BCAAs consume massive NAD⁺ during breakdown, lowering the cytoplasmic NAD⁺/NADH ratio and generating 3-HIB (which drives glucose/triglyceride spikes). High tissue MUFAs indirectly impair BCAA breakdown, causing BCAAs to accumulate. Eliminating isoleucine from high-fat diets completely halts weight gain in mice.
* **The Glycine Counterbalance:** Glycine (from gelatin/collagen) is the direct antidote to BCAA/methionine/tryptophan toxicity.
  * Facilitates **glycine conjugation** to bind and clear toxic acyl-CoA intermediates in urine (reducing acyl-carnitine congestion).
  * Spares NAD⁺, improves gut barrier function (minimizing endotoxin leakage), and decreases fatty acid synthase.
  * Gelatin acts similarly to insulin, blunting postprandial blood sugar spikes.
  * Ancestral protein target: 15% of total calories, with 1/3 to 1/2 consisting of collagen/gelatin to offset muscle meat. A basic daily sufficiency template is 3 eggs plus 30 grams of gelatin.

## 4. Hormones & The Endocrine System

### The Adrenal/Stress Axis
* **Cortisol:** Catabolic hormone driving gluconeogenesis by shredding muscle mass. Chronically elevated cortisol is the primary driver of high blood glucose and insulin resistance in T2D.
* **DHEA & Pregnenolone:** Anabolic, pro-growth cortisol antagonists. Pregnenolone synthesis inside adrenal mitochondria is the rate-limiting step in steroidogenesis. With age, DHEA/progesterone decline while cortisol remains high.
* **Cortisol:DHEA Ratio:** The ultimate marker of biological aging and all-cause mortality. Optimal target is `<0.5` (DHEA at least double cortisol). A ratio `>0.5` is highly correlated with depression; `>1.0` is strongly associated with psychosis and cardiovascular events.
* **Cortisol:Testosterone Ratio:** Dynamic marker of immediate catabolism vs. anabolism. Ratios `>10` virtually guarantee metabolic syndrome.

### Thyroid: The Master Conductor
* **T4 to T3 Conversion:** Inactive T4 is converted to active T3 primarily in the liver. Sabotaged by PUFAs, MUFAs, high cortisol, and nutritional deficiencies (selenium, zinc, magnesium, iodine, D3). Active T3 is required to synthesize pregnenolone from cholesterol.
* **Reverse T3 (rT3):** The metabolic emergency brake. Competes with T3 for cellular receptors without activating them. Elevated by starvation, fasting, and systemic inflammation.
* **Synthetic Dosing (Ray Peat Heuristics):**
  * Mimic natural human secretion ratios (~3:1 or 4:1 T4 to T3).
  * *Frontloading T4:* T4 has a 1-week half-life (takes ~4-6 weeks to reach steady state). Doubling the T4 dose for the first 4 days brings levels to steady state in 5 days. Do *not* frontload T3 (fast-acting).
  * Keep single oral doses of T3 low (no more than 10mcg at a time, max 75mcg/day) and consume with food.

### Androgens vs. Estrogen & Serotonin
* **DHT (Dihydrotestosterone):** Fully saturated terminal metabolite. Cannot aromatize to estrogen. Potent cortisol blocker, serotonin inhibitor, and synergizes with thyroid to raise free T3. Healthy prostate is a high-DHT, low-estrogen environment.
* **Progesterone:** The ultimate protective steroid. Potent 11β-HSD1 inhibitor (reducing active cortisol by 50-60%). Opposes estrogen, degrades serotonin, and is highly thermogenic. Bypasses liver first-pass metabolism when applied to gums (USP powder dissolved in natural d-alpha tocopherol vitamin E). Normal dose is 25-50mg before bed to raise GABA and improve sleep.
* **Estrogen:** Potent reductant that shifts cells toward aerobic glycolysis (Warburg Effect) and away from oxidative phosphorylation. Promoted by PUFA intake, plastics (EDCs), and heavy eccentric exercise.
* **Serotonin:** The "tolerate suffering" hormone. Systemic metabolic inhibitor. High enterocyte-derived serotonin stimulates hepatic stellate cells via 5-HT2B/5-HT2A receptors, driving lipogenesis, fatty liver (NAFLD), and tissue fibrosis. Restricting tryptophan (via gelatin) or using 5-HT2 blockers (Cyproheptadine) releases the metabolic brake.

## 5. Pathology & Modern Disease Models

### Cancer & The Warburg Effect
* Cancer cells overexpress HIF-1a, shut down mitochondrial respiration, and run primitive glucose and glutamine fermentation (generating high lactate and ROS) even in the presence of oxygen. They overexpress GLUT transporters because they are stuck in fat oxidation and starving for glucose.
* **Redox and pH:** Tumor microenvironments are highly reduced (low NAD⁺/NADH), high-lactate, low-CO₂, and highly acidic (low pH). Selective acidification of the internal cancer cell (via high-dose aspirin or 2,6-dihydroxybenzoic acid) triggers apoptosis. Cancer cell cytoplasm is pathologically alkaline, which protects it from programmed cell death.
* **The Vitamin-Aspirin Synergy:** Combining Vitamin B1 (15mg/kg), B3 (30mg/kg), B7 (1.5mg/kg), and Aspirin (15mg/kg) has been shown to lead to complete tumor regression in rodent models. Sourcing a more lipophilic analog, **2,6-dihydroxybenzoic acid** (400mg), demonstrated even higher efficacy. Note that rodents fed a fat-free diet for 2 weeks prior could not be successfully implanted with tumors.

### Insulin Resistance & Diabetes
* Characterized by localized muscle and liver fat accumulation (intramuscular acyl-CoA gridlock). High circulating BCAAs block insulin's ability to promote glucose utilization. Obese individuals exhibit 1/2 to 1/3 the blood flow in adipose tissue compared to lean individuals, creating regional hypoxia.

### Gut Dysbiosis & Leaky Gut
* Mitochondrial damage to colonocytes -> reduced oxygen consumption -> oxygen leaks into the anaerobic deep colon -> death of beneficial obligate anaerobes (e.g., *Akkermansia*) -> thin mucin layer -> overgrowth of oxygen-tolerant facultative anaerobes -> massive endotoxin (LPS) production -> LPS crosses leaky gut -> LPS binds TLR4 -> systemic inflammation and direct NAD⁺ depletion.

### Neurodegeneration
* Alzheimer's ("Type 3 Diabetes") and Parkinson's represent regional manifestations of neuronal energy failure. Neurons do not perform fermentation; if mitochondrial respiration fails, they undergo apoptosis. Amyloid plaques, tau tangles, and Lewy bodies are the tombstones (debris) of dead cells, not the upstream cause.

## 6. Therapeutics, Bioactives & Advanced Protocols

### Foundational Supports
* **Niacinamide (B3):** Direct NAD⁺ precursor. Potent anti-lipolytic. Increases glucose oxidation. Dose: 50-100mg with meals, 3x daily. Avoid nicotinic acid (causes serotonin/histamine release).
* **Aspirin (Acetylsalicylic Acid):** COX and LOX inhibitor. Directly blocks PUFA conversion to inflammatory prostaglandins. Inhibits 11β-HSD1 to lower cortisol. Lowers fat oxidation by up to 30%. Dose: 81-325mg dissolved in warm water with meals. Take 1mg Vitamin K per 325mg aspirin.
* **Thiamine (B1):** Essential cofactor for Pyruvate Dehydrogenase (PDH). 300-500mg thiamine chloride or 100-150mg of lipophilic analogs (e.g., benfotiamine) halves lactate buildup during physical exertion.
* **Biotin (B7):** Cofactor for pyruvate carboxylase. Helps bypass a blocked PDH by redirecting pyruvate into oxaloacetate for gluconeogenesis / energy metabolism.
* **Vitamin E:** PUFA antagonist. Prevents lipid peroxidation. Natural d-alpha-tocopherol (pure D isomer with beta, gamma, delta tocopherols) is required. Take with fat.
* **Magnesium & Zinc:** Cofactors for T4-to-T3 conversion. Magnesium glycinate/malate calms NMDA excitotoxicity and lowers cortisol. Zinc supports androgen synthesis.
* **Taurine:** Calms excitotoxicity, buffers the "jitters" from caffeine/nicotine or MSG.

### Advanced Bioactives
* **Methylene Blue (MB):** Electron cycler. Can unblock all 4 complexes of the ETC (Complex I, II, III, and IV). Acts as an alternative electron cycler, reducing electron backup. Combine with niacinamide to offset MB-induced serotonin increases. Dose: 3-5mg (1% concentration: 1g MB in 4oz bottle).
* **Alpha-Ketoglutarate (α-KG):** Krebs cycle intermediate depleted by excess BCAAs. Dose: 2000mg twice daily to restore insulin sensitivity.
* **R-Alpha-Lipoic Acid (R-ALA):** Dose-dependently increases SIRT1 and NAD⁺/NADH ratio. Dose: 500mg twice daily.
* **Emodin:** Potent 11β-HSD1 inhibitor. Dose: 250-500mg.
* **Cyproheptadine:** Antihistamine and potent 5-HT2 serotonin receptor blocker.
* **Propranolol:** Beta-blocker that lowers adrenaline, preventing excessive stress-induced lipolysis.
* **L-Carnitine:** Increases fat oxidation (use with caution; beneficial primarily to clear mitochondrial traffic jams, but chronically raises fat oxidation).
* **ITPP (Myo-inositol trispyrophosphate):** Binds to hemoglobin, forcing oxygen release (enhancing the Bohr Effect) to treat cellular hypoxia.
* **Catuaba:** Dopaminergic herb that acts as a functional serotonin antagonist to improve motivation and drive.
* **SLU-PP-332:** ERRα inverse agonist that pharmacologically inhibits the "burn fat" hibernation signal, restoring PDH and glucose oxidation.
* **5-Amino-1MQ:** NNMT inhibitor. Blocks the NNMT enzyme from wasting NAD⁺ precursors, preserving cellular NAD⁺ pools to clear reductive stress.
* **Stearoylethanolamide (SEA):** Stearic acid metabolite. Downregulates SCD1, reduces TNF-alpha, and increases metabolic rate. Target: 300mg twice daily (note: 1200mg/day raised Brad Marshall's body temp too excessively).
* **Puerh Tea Extract:** TLR4 blocker. Blocks endotoxin signaling, preventing weight gain on high-fat diets.

### Peptide, EPO & Growth Hormone Protocols
* **Peptide Abbreviations & Codings:** SM5 - semaglutide, 2S10 - ss31, MS5 - mots-c, G5k - HCG.
* **Meldonium and EPO:** Meldonium restricts the entry of fatty acids to the cell, stimulating glucose oxidation. Synergizes with EPO for athletic performance.
* **MOTS-c:** Mitochondrial-derived peptide. Updates metabolic software by improving glucose utilization and insulin sensitivity. Dose: 2.5mg to 5mg, 3x weekly.
* **SS-31 (Elamipretide):** Binds cardiolipin in the inner mitochondrial membrane, stabilizing ETC complexes and preventing electron leakage. Dose: 1mg to 5mg daily. Synergizes with MOTS-c.
* **Tesamorelin:** GHRH analog. Dose: 2mg at night.
* **HGH (Human Growth Hormone):** Direct administrator is potently lipolytic (shreds fat, flooding blood with FFAs, inducing diabetic-like glucose blockade via the Randle Cycle). However, it lowers cortisol by 15-30% and improves skin/healing. Apply EOD (every other day) at night (e.g., 2-4 IU) to reduce insulin sensitivity issues.
* **IGF-1 (IGF-1 LR3):** The preferred anabolic signal. Direct IGF-1 is anti-lipolytic (prevents FFA flood) and enhances glucose oxidation/uptake. Inhibits 11β-HSD1 directly (HGH does not).

## 7. Diagnostics, Measurements, & At-Home Tracking

### Daily Check-Ins
* **Basal Temperature & Pulse:** Waking target: `97.5°F (36.4°C)` with pulse `75-85 bpm`. Midday target (30-40 mins after a high-carb lunch): stable `98.6°F (37.0°C)`. A post-meal *drop* in temperature/pulse indicates a stress-driven metabolism.
* **Continuous Glucose Monitor (CGM):** Track real-time spikes. Run challenges (e.g., standard glucose OGTT vs. glucose + BCAAs vs. glucose + BCAAs + glycine).
* **Methylene Blue Clear Time:** Speed of urinary blueing as a proxy of mitochondrial clearing capacity.
* **Conscious Breathing Exercises:** Decent evidence supports that conscious breathing restructures unconscious breathing over time. Breathe in over your targeted inhale time as deeply as possible while extending your abdomen fully. On the exhale, focus entirely on lowering your abdomen as slowly as possible (exhalation will occur automatically). Hold in a fully exhaled state. Initiate with a hold duration that produces mild air hunger in the second half of a 5-minute block. Once no air hunger is felt across the full 5 minutes, increase the hold time by several seconds.

### Essential Blood Panels
* **TSH:** Optimal target is `<1.0`, ideally `0.4-1.0` (Ray Peat).
* **Free T3 & Free T4:** Target upper quartile (top 25% of reference range).
* **Reverse T3 (rT3):** Target as low as possible.
* **Full Hormone Panel:** Sensitive Estradiol, Progesterone (nmol/L target: 1-4 for males), DHEA-S, Pregnenolone, Prolactin, AM Cortisol, Total & Free Testosterone. Aim for a 5:1 ratio of T to DHT (target total T ~1000-1200 ng/dL for TRT cruising).
* **Metabolic Markers:** Fasting Insulin, HbA1c, hs-CRP, and a Full Iron Panel (including Ferritin - high Ferritin with low serum iron indicates tissue sequestration from active inflammation, not true iron deficiency).

### Advanced Diagnostics
* **Essential & Metabolic Fatty Acids Profile:** Direct measure of red blood cell membrane fats to track PUFA status.
* **Acyl-Carnitine Profile:** Identifies mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation traffic jams (elevated levels indicate FAO blockages/reductive stress).
* **MitoSwab™:** Direct cheek swab functional assay of ETC complexes I-IV.
* **FoodMarble:** Real-time digestive gas monitoring to track small intestinal fermentation.
